# 01 · Strategy — Adoption Roadmap

> Goal: agree on **why** the organization adopts AI, **where** it will create value first, and **who** owns it.

## 1. Maturity stages

| Stage | Description | Typical signals | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · Ad hoc** | Individuals use public AI tools on their own | Shadow usage, no policy, mixed opinions | Leadership agrees AI needs an owner |
| **1 · Explore** | Sanctioned tools, first policy, AI lab running | Approved tool list, first training sessions | Process map done, 2–3 quick wins chosen |
| **2 · Pilot** | Quick wins delivered with baselines | Measured time savings, user feedback | ≥1 pilot meets its success criteria |
| **3 · Scale** | Repeatable delivery pipeline for use cases | Use-case backlog, shared platform, gateway | AI use cases delivered like any other feature |
| **4 · Embed** | AI is part of how processes are designed | AI considered in every new requirement | ROI reported quarterly to management |

Most enterprises sit at stage 0–1. **Don't skip stages** — skipping governance or measurement is what kills programs at stage 3.

## 2. 12-month roadmap

| Quarter | Focus | Key deliverables |
|---|---|---|
| **Q1 · Foundations** | Ownership, policy, discovery | AI owner appointed · usage policy v1 · approved tool list · process map of 2–3 departments · AI lab running |
| **Q2 · Quick wins** | Deliver and measure | 2–3 pilots live with baselines · first training wave · first ROI report |
| **Q3 · Platform** | Make delivery repeatable | Shared AI platform/gateway · use-case lifecycle adopted · second training wave · full process map |
| **Q4 · Scale** | Expand and institutionalize | 5–10 use cases in production · AI in requirements templates · annual review & next-year plan |

## 3. Operating model

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Executive sponsor** | Owns the budget and removes blockers; receives quarterly ROI report |
| **AI adoption lead** | Owns the roadmap, backlog and governance process; single point of contact |
| **AI lab / platform team** | Evaluates models and tools, runs the shared platform, sets technical standards |
| **Department champions** | One per department; surfaces processes, validates value, drives local adoption |
| **Security / legal / privacy** | Co-owns the policy; reviews high-risk use cases |
| **Systems analysts** | Translate business problems into AI use-case specifications |

**Start small:** in many organizations the lead, one or two engineers and a champion per department are enough for year one.


## Anti-patterns

- **"Licenses for everyone" as the strategy.** Usage spikes, then fades, with no measurable outcome.
- **Innovation theater.** Many demos, zero production use cases.
- **Central team does everything.** Without department champions, the backlog dries up.
- **Waiting for the perfect policy.** Ship v1 in weeks, iterate quarterly.
